### 2.11.6 The Residue, Located

§2.11.5 stated the conjecture and named its two instances. Between that statement and this section, the conjecture was taken as a working hypothesis and pushed, in a pre-registered, citation-checked program, toward the one thing it had never been asked to do: say *where* the computational layer's residue actually sits, precisely enough that a proof could be aimed at it. The program proved no theorem toward P vs NP. What it produced is a map — and the map corrects the conjecture in three places while confirming it in one. Those corrections are the content of this section.

**The residue is about the typical object, never the named one.** This is the finding that recurs, and it recurs at every layer examined. At the level of instances: any argument that derives a contradiction from a specific compressible string — a Gödel-style diagonal instance, a sampled output of a short program, a set of low-Kolmogorov-complexity strings — bears only on worst-case computation, because such strings have measure at most $2^{k+1-n}$ under the uniform distribution, which is inside the error budget that average-case hardness permits (`pvsnp/R2`). At the level of proof systems: a system can prove strong lower bounds against *itself* on a structured tautology family (Davis–Robere 2026, for bounded-depth Frege on the pigeonhole principle; Garlík 2019 for resolution), yet no proof system efficiently proves that *random* truth tables lack short hardness proofs (Pich–Santhanam 2019). At the level of theories: the diagonal can build strong-but-cheap extensions — a Rosser-style fixpoint the stronger theory cannot prove, whose added consistency the weaker system nevertheless proves in polynomial size (Hrubeš, in Pudlák 2017) — so that the finite Gödel conjecture must be stated over *all* proofs of a system (its reflection principle), not over any named sentence it lacks. At the bottom of the proof-system ladder: consistency — the statement about one named nontautology, $\bot$ — is provable by resolution in polynomial size, while reflection — soundness over all proofs — is not (Pudlák 2020, Prop. 3.9; Atserias–Bonet). Six verified instances, one pattern: the objects a layer can *name* are the objects it can reason about, and the residue lives on the objects no short description picks out. §2.11.3's Mechanism 2 said "hard functions look random to the layer." The corrected statement is stronger and less mysterious: hardness is a property of the layer's *typical* objects, and typicality is exactly what a diagonal construction — which produces a named object — cannot reach.

**Gödel's move, applied to hardness, is a restatement, not a lever.** §2.11.3 read Razborov's bounded-arithmetic result as Mechanism 2 "converting into" Mechanism 1. The program tested the conversion in the direction a proof would need — from unprovability to truth — and found it empty. For a hardness sentence $H$ at a fixed resource bound (a $\Pi_1$ sentence) and any $\Sigma_1$-sound theory $T$: $T \nvdash \neg H$ if and only if $H$ is true (`pvsnp/M3`, proved). "Unprovable from inside, hence true" says nothing a lower bound does not already say. Gödel's actual lever was never that inference; it was the diagonal fixed point — a sentence asserting its own unprovability, so that unprovability follows from *consistency alone*, without knowing truth. Hardness sentences have no such fixed point. The propositional analogue does exist — a tautology asserting that it has no short proof, hard for any sound system by soundness alone — but it must contain the excluded proof as variables, so it is as large as what it names, and the resulting bound is at most polynomially above the trivial one (`pvsnp/M4`, proved; this is why Pudlák's finitistic consistency statements have polynomially separated upper and lower bounds). Compressing the self-reference through implicit proofs changes the *measure*, not the *ratio*: the free lunch that would follow — superpolynomial lower bounds for every sound system, hence NP ≠ coNP — does not exist, and the reason is located. The finite second incompleteness theorem is false as a universal statement: Frege systems prove their own reflection principles in polynomial size (Buss), and so do all systems above them. Only weak systems cannot, and the divide is expressiveness — whether a system's lines can state "this encoded line is satisfied by this assignment" — not reuse, and not self-encoding as such.

**Escape by extension between systems is just separation.** §2.11.4 predicted that a layer's boundary object is settled one layer up. At the level of proof systems this is exactly true and exactly empty: a system that cheaply proves a stronger system's reflection principle p-simulates it (`pvsnp/A2`, proved, by a classical method due to Cook), so "S cannot prove T's soundness" carries no content beyond "T is stronger than S." The only statement in Pudlák's family with content for *every* system is that no optimal proof system exists — no single layer simulates all layers — and that statement implies NP ≠ coNP, relativizes in both directions (Ben-David–Gringauze 1998; Khaniki 2019), has no known non-relativizing partial result, and, unlike NP ≠ coNP itself, has no agreed direction of belief. The residue at the metamathematical layer is barrier-blocked in the same way as the object-level problem. That is self-similarity of the obstruction across layers — consistency with the conjecture, and not progress toward the theorem.

**Where the conjecture is confirmed, it is a theorem — in the black-box world.** The intuition behind §2.11 is that problems fold into problems and the folds accumulate until unfolding is impossible; Gödel numbering is such a fold, the meta-level folded into the object level. Taken as *nesting* rather than as composition — one quantifier, one layer of circuit depth per fold — this is proved wherever it can be checked. The arithmetical hierarchy is strict (Tarski, Kleene). Depth-$(d{+}1)$ circuits cannot be unfolded into depth $d$ without exponential cost (Håstad). And the polynomial hierarchy is infinite relative to a random oracle (Rossman–Servedio–Tan 2015). The exact line where the theorem stops is one sentence, due to Book (1994): *relative to oracles, accumulation is what is proved; only collapse ever transfers down to the unrelativized world.* The white-box form of "folds accumulate" is the strictness of the polynomial hierarchy, which implies P ≠ NP. The conjecture's prediction is therefore correct in every setting where the evaluator is unbounded or the fold is opaque, and its unproved form is the problem itself.

**The smallest open instance, with its obstruction named.** Pushed to the smallest fold whose addition is open for an explicit function, the map ends at a single question in circuit complexity: does the inner-product-mod-2 function have polynomial-size depth-two threshold circuits with unrestricted weights (Kane–Williams 2016, open question 2)? For majority gates the third fold provably adds; for arbitrary weights it is open, and the sub-structure is known: it would follow from the sign-rank of every function in that class being $2^{o(n)}$ (Chattopadhyay–Mande 2022, §8 — their own open question), which in turn implies that inner product has no short decision list of exact-threshold gates, which would follow from inner product lying outside a communication class they show needs new techniques. Every known measure fails at the same function, **Equality** — $x = y$, the diagonal of the communication matrix: its 1-rectangles are single points, its short lists have exponential sign-rank, and it escapes discrepancy even as a list (`pvsnp/X1`, `X2`). The lemma nobody has is a communication measure that is small on short lists of Equalities and exponential on inner product. At this layer, moreover, "a fold" is measured by the sign-rank exponent rather than by Boolean depth: unrestricted weights absorb Boolean nesting outright (a three-level Boolean function has a linear-size two-level threshold circuit), and weight buys a strict *partial* fold if and only if Chattopadhyay–Mande's question has the expected answer.

**What this does to the conjecture.** Three of §2.11.5's phrases must be read more narrowly than they were written. "Resistant to the layer's own constructive, reusable methods" is correct for the typical object and false for named ones: layers routinely settle their own limits on structured instances. "By diagonal fixed-point" is a mechanism of the arithmetic layer that, at the computational layer, yields only polynomial gaps. And "resolution requires re-encoding within a strictly richer lattice" is, between proof systems, the definition of a richer lattice rather than a prediction about it. What survives is sharper than what was claimed: the residue of a self-encoding layer is the set of statements about its *typical* objects, and every mechanism examined — density, expressiveness, relativization — is a form of the single fact that a short description names an atypical thing. The conjecture's confirmation is the black-box theorem; its open form is P vs NP; and its smallest checkable instance is one question about the diagonal.

### Insertion for §3.3.2 (after "Two layers, two mechanisms, one trigger…")

One qualification, learned by trying to use the pattern rather than by stating it. The residue is not any hard question the layer can pose about itself; layers settle many of those. It is specifically the set of questions about the layer's *typical* objects — the ones no short description inside the layer picks out. A self-model can, and does, prove its own limits on the instances it can name; what it cannot reach is what it cannot name. That is why the hard problem feels like a *residue* rather than a puzzle: what remains after every nameable aspect of experience has been modeled is exactly the unnameable part, and it remains for the same reason a proof system cannot prove that random truth tables are hard (ch2 §2.11.6).

### Insertion for §10.5.4.3 (after "…the lattice itself extended, and the boundary object settled from outside what used to be the whole of you.")

A caution from the formal side, because the metaphor of "walking through the door" can be read as promising more than it delivers. Between two proof systems, settling the weaker one's boundary object from the stronger one turns out to be *the same fact* as the stronger one being stronger (ch2 §2.11.6): the extension is not a method for settling the residue, it is what settling the residue is called. The mind's meta-gap is resolved by acquiring the frame that resolves it — but that sentence, read carefully, says only that the resolution and the new frame are one event, which is exactly what §10.5.4.3 claims and no more. The productive content is elsewhere: in the fact that the new frame exists to be acquired at all, which for minds is an empirical observation and for proof systems is the open question of whether any system is optimal.
