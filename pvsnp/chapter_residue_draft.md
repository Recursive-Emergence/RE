### 2.11.6 The Residue, Located

§2.11.5 stated the conjecture and named its two instances. Between that statement and this section, the conjecture was taken as a working hypothesis and pushed, in a pre-registered, citation-checked program, toward the one thing it had never been asked to do: say *where* the computational layer's residue actually sits, precisely enough that a proof could be aimed at it. The program proved no theorem toward P vs NP. What it produced is a map — and the map narrows the conjecture in three places while confirming it in one. Those corrections are the content of this section.

**A reading that recurred: the residue is about the typical object, never the named one.** This is a reading, not a theorem — the program labels it so — but it was observed six times in verified results, and it is the one pattern that appeared at every layer examined. (1) At the level of instances: any argument that derives a contradiction from a specific compressible string — a Gödel-style diagonal instance, a sampled output of a short program, a set of low-Kolmogorov-complexity strings — bears only on worst-case computation, because such strings have measure at most $2^{k+1-n}$ under the uniform distribution, inside the error budget that average-case hardness permits (`pvsnp/R2`, proved). (2) At the level of adversaries, the same density fact is two-sided: because compressible strings are few, an *errorless* heuristic that rejects most random strings becomes a usable test that a non-black-box reduction can exploit by reading its source code (Hirahara 2018), and the same fewness lets a *two-sided-error* heuristic hide all its mistakes on the compressible set, which is exactly where that technique stops (`pvsnp/R4`–`R5`, verified). (3) At the level of tautologies: a proof system can prove strong lower bounds against *itself* on a structured family — bounded-depth Frege on the pigeonhole principle (Davis–Robere 2026) — while resolution cannot on its own refutation statements (Garlík 2019), a contrast by system rather than by structure; and, for *most* functions, no proof system efficiently proves that the function is hard, i.e. has no small circuits (Pich–Santhanam 2019). (4) At the level of theories: the diagonal can build strong-but-cheap extensions — a Rosser-style fixpoint the stronger theory cannot prove, whose added consistency the weaker system nevertheless proves in polynomial size (Hrubeš, in Pudlák 2017) — so the finite Gödel conjecture must be stated over *all* proofs of a system (its reflection principle), not over a named sentence it lacks. (5) At the bottom of the proof-system ladder: consistency — a statement about one named nontautology, $\bot$ — is provable by resolution in polynomial size, while reflection — soundness over all proofs — is not (Pudlák 2020, Prop. 3.9; Atserias–Bonet). (6) At the metamathematical layer, the residue relativizes in both directions exactly as the object-level problem does (`pvsnp/A4`), which the program counts as *consistency* with the reading and not as evidence for it. Read together: the objects a layer can name are the objects it can reason about, and at each of the six places counted, what the program found on the far side of the obstruction was the typical object no short description picks out. (Two other divides the program met are *not* counted: the self-reflection divide between weak and strong proof systems, which the record files under expressiveness, and the relativization of the metamathematical residue, which it files as consistency only.) §2.11.3's Mechanism 2 said "hard functions look random to the layer." The reading sharpens that: the residue is a statement about the layer's *typical* objects, and a diagonal construction — which produces a *named* object — is the wrong tool by construction. Whether this is a theorem-shaped fact or a coincidence of six cases is open; the program did not turn it into a statement.

**Gödel's move, applied to hardness, is a restatement, not a lever.** §2.11.3 read Razborov's bounded-arithmetic result as Mechanism 2 "converting into" Mechanism 1. The program tested the conversion in the direction a proof would need — from unprovability to truth — and found it empty. For a hardness sentence $H$ at a fixed resource bound (a $\Pi_1$ sentence) and any theory $T$ that contains Robinson's Q and is $\Sigma_1$-sound: $T \nvdash \neg H$ if and only if $H$ is true (`pvsnp/M3`, proved). "Unprovable from inside, hence true" says nothing a lower bound does not already say. Gödel's actual lever was never that inference; it was the diagonal fixed point — a sentence asserting its own unprovability, so that unprovability follows from *consistency alone*, without knowing truth. Hardness sentences have no such fixed point. The propositional analogue does exist — a tautology asserting that it has no short proof, hard for any sound system by soundness alone — but it must contain the excluded proof as variables, so it is as large as what it names, and the resulting bound is at most polynomially above the trivial one (`pvsnp/M4`, proved). Pudlák's finitistic consistency statements are verified to have upper and lower bounds "only polynomially distant" (Pudlák 1986); the program's bound is the same phenomenon in propositional form, and no causal claim beyond that is made. Compressing the self-reference through implicit proofs changes the *measure*, not the *ratio* (`pvsnp/M4`, proved), so the argument that free compression would yield superpolynomial lower bounds for every sound system has no force. And a finite second incompleteness theorem in the universal self form — "no proof system efficiently proves its own soundness" — cannot hold as worded: Frege systems prove their own reflection principles in polynomial size (Buss), as do extensions of Circuit Frege and the systems $G_i$ (verified in `pvsnp/A3`); resolution provably does not (Atserias–Bonet), cutting planes does not as stated by Pudlák 2020; bounded-depth Frege and $\mathrm{Res}(k)$ are open. What divides the two sides is not reuse — that reading was tested and falsified on the Frege row — and the program's post hoc, unconfirmed candidate is expressiveness: whether a system's lines can state "this encoded line is satisfied by this assignment."

**Escape by extension between systems, where it was tested, is separation.** §2.11.4 predicted that a layer's boundary object is settled one layer up. At the level of proof systems the program found this true and empty in the one setting where it proved anything: for systems closed under restriction and literal substitution ($\mathrm{Res}(k)$), a short refutation of a stronger system $T$'s reflection statement yields short refutations of every formula $T$ refutes shortly (`pvsnp/A2`, Lemma 3, proved — a size bound, by the classical method due to Cook; the general-system form is cited, not re-proved). So, in that setting, "S cannot prove T's soundness" carries no content beyond "T is stronger than S." The one statement in Pudlák's family with content for *every* system is that no optimal proof system exists — no single layer simulates all layers — and that statement implies NP ≠ coNP, relativizes in both directions (Ben-David–Gringauze 1998; Khaniki 2019, verified from the constructions), has no known non-relativizing partial result, and, unlike NP ≠ coNP itself, has no agreed direction of belief. The residue at the metamathematical layer is barrier-blocked in the same way as the object-level problem. That is consistency with the conjecture, not progress toward the theorem.

**Where the conjecture is confirmed, it is a theorem — in the black-box world.** The intuition behind §2.11 is that problems fold into problems and the folds accumulate until unfolding is impossible; Gödel numbering is such a fold, the meta-level folded into the object level. Taken as *nesting* rather than as composition — one quantifier, one layer of circuit depth per fold — this is proved in the cases the program could verify. The arithmetical hierarchy does not collapse: Tarski's undefinability is sourced (Pudlák 2020), and strictness via Post's theorem is recorded as secondary. Depth-$(d{+}1)$ circuits cannot be unfolded into depth $d$ without exponential cost (Håstad). And the polynomial hierarchy is infinite relative to a random oracle (Rossman–Servedio–Tan 2015). The line where the theorem stops is one sentence, due to Book (1994), and it is the one known transfer between the two worlds for random oracles: *relative to a random oracle, accumulation is what is proved; what is known to transfer down to the unrelativized world is collapse.* The white-box form of "folds accumulate" is the strictness of the polynomial hierarchy, which implies P ≠ NP. The conjecture's prediction is therefore confirmed in the proved cases — unbounded evaluators, and depth in the circuit classes where separations are theorems (the AC⁰ depth hierarchy; the majority-gate fold from depth two to three, via inner product; the unconditional strictness of the depth-two threshold chain) — and its unproved form is the problem itself. (Two neighbouring cases were not established by the program: strictness of the log-time hierarchy, and a depth hierarchy for constant-depth circuits with modular counting gates.)

**The smallest open instance, with its obstruction named.** Pushed to the smallest fold whose addition is open for an explicit function, the map ends at a single question in circuit complexity: does the inner-product-mod-2 function have polynomial-size depth-two threshold circuits with unrestricted weights (Kane–Williams 2016, open question 2)? For majority gates the third fold provably adds; for arbitrary weights it is open, and the sub-structure is known: it would follow from the sign-rank of every function in that class being $2^{o(n)}$ (Chattopadhyay–Mande 2022, §8 — their own open question), which in turn implies that inner product has no short decision list of exact-threshold gates, which would follow from inner product lying outside a communication class for which, in their words, new techniques are suggested. Every known measure fails at the same function, **Equality** — $x = y$, the diagonal of the communication matrix: its 1-rectangles are single points, its short lists have exponential sign-rank, and it escapes discrepancy even as a list (`pvsnp/X1`, `X2`). The lemma nobody has is a communication measure that is small on short lists of Equalities and exponential on inner product. At this layer, moreover, "a fold" is measured by the sign-rank exponent rather than by Boolean depth: unrestricted weights absorb Boolean nesting outright (a three-level Boolean function has a linear-size two-level threshold circuit), and the strictness of that absorption is unconditional (Chattopadhyay–Mande). What is conditional on their open question is only whether the fold weight buys on inner product is *partial* rather than full.

**What this does to the conjecture.** Three of §2.11.5's phrases must be read more narrowly than they were written. "Resistant to the layer's own constructive, reusable methods" is, on the evidence, a claim about typical objects and not about named ones: layers do settle their own limits on structured instances. "By diagonal fixed-point" is a mechanism of the arithmetic layer that, at the computational layer, yields only polynomial gaps. And "resolution requires re-encoding within a strictly richer lattice" is, between the proof systems where it was tested, the definition of a richer lattice rather than a prediction about it. What survives is narrower and more usable than what was claimed: in the six counted cases, the residue of a self-encoding layer is a statement about its *typical* objects; the conjecture's confirmation is the black-box theorem; its open form is P vs NP; and its smallest checkable instance is one question about the diagonal.

### Insertion for §3.3.2 (after "Two layers, two mechanisms, one trigger…")

One qualification, learned by trying to use the pattern rather than by stating it. The residue is not any hard question the layer can pose about itself; layers settle many of those. In the six cases the program counted, it is specifically a question about the layer's *typical* objects — the ones no short description inside the layer picks out. A self-model can sometimes prove its own limits on the instances it can name (bounded-depth Frege does; resolution does not); what none of them reaches is what it cannot name. By analogy — and it is offered as an analogy, not a derivation — the hard problem feels like a *residue* rather than a puzzle because what remains after every nameable aspect of experience has been modeled is exactly the unnameable part; the formal cousin is the fact that, for most functions, no proof system efficiently proves the function hard (ch2 §2.11.6).

### Insertion for §10.5.4.3 (after "…the lattice itself extended, and the boundary object settled from outside what used to be the whole of you.")

A caution from the formal side, because the metaphor of "walking through the door" can be read as promising more than it delivers. Between proof systems of the kind where it was tested, a stronger system being stronger already *entails* that the weaker one cannot cheaply prove the stronger one's soundness (ch2 §2.11.6, one direction, proved for resolution-type systems; whether a system can prove its *own* soundness is a separate question, open for the weak systems that matter here): the extension is not an independent method for settling the residue; settling it is part of what the extension being stronger means. The mind's meta-gap is resolved by acquiring the frame that resolves it — but that sentence, read carefully, says only that the resolution and the new frame are one event, which is exactly what §10.5.4.3 claims and no more. The productive content is elsewhere: in the fact that the new frame exists to be acquired at all, which for minds is an empirical observation and for proof systems is the open question of whether any system is optimal.
